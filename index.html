01 — About me

A student, still figuring things out in public.

I'm Miheer Nayak — a frontend and backend developer in training, currently in my first semester of B.Tech Computer Science at Karnavati University. I finished class 10 under CBSE with 84.2% and class 12 in the PCM stream under RBSE with 86.6%, then sat JEE Main and landed in the 90th percentile. My JEE Advanced score wasn't what I hoped for. I'm mentioning it anyway, because that result taught me more about how I handle a setback than any of my good marks did.

I'm now in my first semester of B.Tech Computer Science at Karnavati University. Most of what I know about programming so far came from sitting with a compiler and getting things wrong until they worked — not from a lecture slide. C taught me that memory is a real thing you have to manage. JavaScript taught me that the same logic can turn into something a person can actually click on. And yes — my college teaches C on Turbo C++, a compiler older than I am. I've made my peace with it, and it did teach me to save before running.

Outside class, I read about AI — the honest, technical version, not the headlines. FAI is on my timetable this semester and it's the subject I think about longest after class. My only credential there is that I keep showing up to it. I'd rather show you an honest learning curve than claim a mastery I haven't earned yet.

"I'm early in this. But I'm not standing still, and that's the part I can prove."

I play cricket, which is probably where my patience comes from — I bowl, so I've had plenty of practice at being hit. Long formats teach you that a bad over doesn't end the match, and neither does a bad semester.

08 — Questions I get asked

The awkward ones, answered.

Isn't AI a bit much for first year?

FAI is literally on my timetable, so I don't have much choice. But the part I actually care about isn't the hype — it's understanding how a machine reaches a decision, and being able to explain that without using the word "magic". If I'm still confused in a year, at least it'll be a more interesting kind of confusion.

Why C, in 2026?

Because it hides nothing from me. When I learned pointers, I actually learned what a variable is. I'd rather start at the bottom and work upwards than start with something convenient and never find out what's underneath it.

Why does cricket keep appearing on a computer science portfolio?

Because it's the one thing I've done for years without quitting, and it's where I learned to be bad at something in front of other people and keep going anyway. As a bowler you get about six attempts an over to be wrong in public. That is exactly the skill programming asks for.

Turbo C++? In 2026?

Believe me, I know. My college teaches C on it, so that's where I learnt C — and honestly, having to save before every run and watching a console window shut instantly taught me to read error messages properly. I still use it for coursework. Everything else I write, I write in VS Code.

Frontend or backend?

Both, on purpose. Frontend is where I get to see the result instantly — this site is the proof. Backend is where the logic actually lives, and it's the part I understand least right now, which is exactly why I'm not dropping it. I'd say I'm the stronger frontend developer today because I've shipped more of it; the cricket game will need both, which is the point.

What would you want from an internship?

Something small and real, an honest review when I get it wrong, and a team that explains why rather than just what. I'd rather fix my mistakes early than carry them for three years.

09 — Build process

From a blank file to this page.

Seven steps. No framework, no template, no page builder — about 1,600 lines of HTML, CSS and JavaScript written by hand, one section at a time.

Step 01

Blank file

Started with an empty document and one question: what should this feel like before it says anything?

Step 02

Structure first, design later

Listed every section as plain text, in order. Cut four of them for being filler.

Step 03

Content

Turned my own notes into sentences, with AI as an editor rather than an author. Every fact is mine.

Step 04

Design system

Midnight and teal, one serif for display, one sans for reading. Every colour after that had to earn its place.

Step 05

Build

Hand-wrote the layout, the scroll reveals, the skill meters and the mobile navigation in vanilla JS.

Step 06

Test

Checked at 360px, 768px and 1440px wide. Fixed two real bugs: the mobile menu and the anchor scrolling.

Step 07

Ship

Pushed to GitHub Pages, then went back and rewrote the copy twice because it still sounded like a brochure.
